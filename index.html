Extended Electrodynamics · SLW

How the Hively Bifilar Coil
Receiving Antenna Works

Why a specially wound bifilar coil produces an output current from an incident longitudinally polarized electric field (or vector potential) — with zero magnetic field.

Q The Question

Why does the Hively bifilar coiled receiving antenna deliver an output current due to the incident longitudinally polarized electric field and/or the incident vector potential wave?

1 Core Idea in One Sentence

The bifilar coil is geometrically matched to the irrotational (gradient-driven) character of the incident longitudinal fields. A longitudinal electric field \( \mathbf{E}_{L} \) (or the associated longitudinal vector potential \( \mathbf{A}_{L} \)) therefore drives a net current, while the magnetic field remains essentially zero.

2 Core Physics from Extended Electrodynamics (EED)

In Extended Electrodynamics (Hively and collaborators), the Helmholtz decomposition is taken seriously: any vector field splits into a solenoidal (transverse, divergence-free) part and an irrotational (longitudinal, curl-free) part. Classical electrodynamics largely discards or gauges away the irrotational sector; EED retains it.

⚡
An irrotational current density \( \mathbf{J}_{L} = \nabla j \) produces (and is produced by) an irrotational vector potential \( \mathbf{A}_{L} = \nabla a \) and a longitudinal electric field \( \mathbf{E}_{L} \).
∅
The associated magnetic field vanishes: \( \mathbf{B} = \nabla \times \mathbf{A}_{L} = 0 \).
🌊
The dynamical scalar field \( C = \nabla \cdot \mathbf{A} + \frac{1}{c^{2}}\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t} \) is nonzero and travels with the longitudinal \( \mathbf{E} \)-field, forming the scalar-longitudinal wave (SLW). The wave carries energy and momentum without a magnetic component and is unconstrained by the classical skin effect.

Constitutive Relation

Inside a conductor (low-frequency / quasi-static limit):

\[ \mathbf{J}_{L} \approx \sigma \mathbf{E}_{L} \]

Because \( \mathbf{E}_{L} \) is itself a gradient (or the time derivative of a gradient potential), the driven current is likewise irrotational.

3 Why the Bifilar Geometry Responds

The Hively bifilar (or bifilar-pancake) coil is wound so that the two conductors run side-by-side (or in alternating turns) and are connected at the center (or appropriately terminated) such that any current that does flow travels in opposite directions in adjacent turns.

Simplified schematic of a bifilar coil. Blue and cyan traces represent the two interleaved conductors. Current flows in opposite directions in neighboring turns, cancelling the net magnetic field.

Magnetic Cancellation

Opposing currents cancel the net magnetic moment and the net inductance. The coil cannot support ordinary eddy currents or respond inductively to a transverse \( \mathbf{B} \)-field.

Gradient Response

What remains is a pure gradient response. An incident longitudinal \( \mathbf{E}_{L} \) (or \( -\partial\mathbf{A}_{L}/\partial t \)) imposes a potential gradient along the conductors, driving a coherent irrotational current to the terminals.

🎯
No opposing eddy-current loops cancel the signal, because there is no incident \( \mathbf{B} \) to induce them. The coil is a geometric filter matched to the irrotational sector.

Ordinary loop or dipole antennas respond primarily to the solenoidal (\( \mathbf{B} \)- and transverse-\( \mathbf{E} \)) sector. The bifilar coil is essentially blind to that sector and open to the longitudinal sector.

4 Vector-Potential Picture

Even if one prefers to speak in potentials rather than fields, the same conclusion follows. An incident longitudinal vector potential \( \mathbf{A}_{L} = \nabla a \) (curl-free) produces an electric field:

\[ \mathbf{E}_{L} = -\nabla\Phi - \frac{\partial\mathbf{A}_{L}}{\partial t} \]

Inside the conductors the charges rearrange (or a current flows) until the total force vanishes in the steady state, or until a continuous current is sustained by the time-varying part. Because the bifilar geometry forces any current to be counter-directed in adjacent turns, the only consistent solution is the gradient-driven (irrotational) current that the coil was designed to accept.

Thus the observed output current is the direct macroscopic manifestation of the incident longitudinally polarized electric field (or its underlying longitudinal vector potential) acting on a structure whose topology admits only irrotational current.

5 Summary

Key Takeaways

  • SLWs possess a longitudinal \( \mathbf{E} \)-field and a dynamical scalar field \( C \), but no magnetic field.
  • The bifilar winding forces currents in opposite directions → net \( \mathbf{B} \approx 0 \) and near-zero inductance.
  • This leaves the coil sensitive only to gradient-driven (irrotational) currents produced by \( \mathbf{E}_{L} \) or \( \partial\mathbf{A}_{L}/\partial t \).
  • Result: a measurable terminal current appears when an SLW is incident, even though classical inductive coupling is suppressed.
